## Introduction

• Today is Global Mission Sunday. When we talk about a "mission" we're talking about going out to achieve some goal. The goal Jesus gave his disciples was to spread the news of the gospel, to be his witnesses to the ends of the earth. To make disciples of all nations. It's a goal that can't be confined to one area. We want more people to know God—in Carlingford, yes—but not just in Carlingford. We want all people everyone to know Jesus.

• So today we will lift our eyes to what God is doing through His people to make Jesus known to the ends of the earth.

• If you're here today and you don't really know Jesus, I hope this gives you a helpful glimpse into why we as Christians love Jesus and want more people to know Him.

• If you are a Christian, I hope after today you'll be able to take home at least one practical next step about how to support God's global mission.

• Psalm 65 (focusing on v 1–5) isn't a manual on global mission; doesn't give us the imperatives, the tactics, the instructions. What it does give us is it points us to the foundation for mission—God Himself.


## God is worthy of all praise (v1)

• Statement: Psalm 65 begins with a vision of praise. The author imagines a world where God arrives and is greeted by praise.

• Passage: v1: "Praise awaits you, our God, in Zion; to you our vows will be fulfilled."

• Explanation: Some people get confused about praise. Some think that God is sitting around waiting for praise; that he is praise-hungry. But the Bible clearly says that God doesn't need our praise; he is not served by humans hands; he does not need anything from us. What we have in this verse is not a desperate God wanting praise from people; but rather a desperate people waiting to praise God and hungry to see more people join the song. This is what a missionary is, and this is what a Christian is: someone who loves God and wants others to join them in their love for God.

• Illustration: Have you ever met a great missionary for something other than God? Their goal is to show you something—it could be anything—a great restaurant, a great scene, a great meme, a great person that you must meet, a great book you must read, a great song you must hear, a great food you must taste.

• Application #1: Great missionaries and great evangelists take on many different styles and approaches. Some people say they'll never be a missionary because they don't match the style of so-and-so. But do you know what all great missionaries have in common? A deep, personal love for the what they want to share, a care for the person they are sharing it with. That's the key to mission work, that's the key to evangelism, that's the key to supporting missionaries and evangelists: the key to mission is loving God. It makes sense doesn't it? The more you worship something, the more you'll want to share it with others so they too can experience its goodness.

---- Quote (Piper, Let The Nations Be Glad): '"Mission begins and ends in worship ... Mission is not the ultimate goal of the church. Worship is. Missions exists because worship doesn’t. Worship is ultimate, not missions, because God is ultimate, not man. When this age is over and the countless millions of the redeemed fall on their faces before the throne of God, missions will be no more. It is a temporary necessity. But worship abides forever."

• Why is God worthy of all praise? ... --> We get three reasons in the next three verses: God answers, God forgives, and God brings near.

### God answers (v2)

• Statement: God is worthy of praise because He is the only one who answers the prayers of his creatures.

• Passage: v2: "You who answer prayer, to you all people will come."

• Explanation: One of the most painful things in life is a person who you need to help you who refuses to listen to you. The world is full of idols that people pour themselves out to, but they are deaf; but God hears prayer—and so all people will come to Him when they realise that.

• Illustration: James Fraser was a missionary to Southwestern China in the early part of the 20th century. He met a people who were fearful of spirits and unsure about the gods they had made for themselves; they performed rituals and rites but had no confidence that any god would actually do anything for them. When Fraser told them about Jesus' life and death and resurrection they were completely in awe of the mercy of God and so asked Fraser to speak to Jesus to see if Jesus might show them mercy too. Fraser said to them, "I don't need to speak to God, because you can speak to God and he will hear you." And with tears they prayed to the God who made them; it was the first time they had tasted the joy of speaking to God with confidence that he was listening.

• Illustration: Lachlan and Candy Grice in Taiwan. They wrote a few months ago: "Our culture class visited a temple - it was informative and very sad. The camera hasn't done justice here to the amount of gold in the Jade Emperor's room. We were told that not many people deem themselves important enough to directly pray to the busy Jade Emperor. Instead, they will bring their requests through lesser gods who have more time for them."

• Application: We have a God who listens and answers because he loves us and cares. Let us therefore pray to Him without hesitation and with great confidence that He can answer.

• Transition: We long for more people to come to the true God. How do we know the God of the Bible has time for people; answers prayer? After all—He doesn't need to; and in fact there is good reason for Him not to. Part of the reason Christian prayer is so revolutionary for people all over the world is because they assume there is a barrier between them and God which removes all hope; but their assumptions are smashed when they realise God has broken down that dividing wall of hostility through the death of His son.

### God forgives (v3)

• Statement: God is worthy of praise because He breaks down the barrier between us and Him; he addresses our biggest problem: our sin.

• Passage: V3: "When we were overwhelmed by sins, you forgave our transgressions."

• Explanation: Sin is overwhelming because it puts us in opposition to God—no person can live like that. We weren't made to live like that. But in God's kindness, He forgives us, and this blessing is open to the whole world.

• Illustration: It's unbearable to be trapped under the water while waves roll overhead. Why? Because we weren't made to live underwater; we need oxygen. It's unbearable to live life trapped in sin and seperated from God. Why? Because we weren't made to live in sin; we need God. There are billions of people in our world trapped under the waves and the weight of sin who need rescue. Best estimates have it at over 5 billion people in the world without saving faith in Jesus. Around 2–3 billion of these are what we would call "unreached", people who don't know Jesus at all.

• Application: missionaries are normal people, and in the course of normal life can get distracted from the mission. Sometimes you see this in prayer newsletters about sickness or holidays. One thing we can do is to remind ourselves and them of the incredible mission we've been called into. That is to say, whenever we hear about mission prayer points, our instinct should be not just to pray for the missionary, but for the mission itself—for forgiveness of sin, for changed lives, people moving from death to life.

### God brings near (v4)

• Transition / statement: God is sovereign and He is not done bringing people to Himself.

• Passage: V4: "Blessed are those you choose and bring near to live in your courts! We are filled with the good things of your house, of your holy temple."

• Explain: Part of the joy of being involved in God's mission in the world is knowing that at the end of the day it is God who chooses people, God who changes people, and God who brings people to Himself.

• Illustrate:

• Application: There are many temples in our world that leave people feeling utterly empty. There are temples in our culture too—more subtle. Places that can become places of worship if that is where people go to find fullness, but inevitably leave empty. The office that becomes a temple to success; the gym that becomes a temple to fitness; the school that becomes a temple to achievement—and yet these places when they become temples leave people empty. After Lachlan and Candy wrote about their temple visit in Taiwan, they wrote a prayer point which read: "Pray that many in Taiwan would see the vanity of idolatry and turn to Jesus as the true and only God, who alone can offer true blessing and security." Might this be our prayer not only for Taiwan but for the whole world!

## God is the hope of the ends of the earth (v5)

How does God bring people in? He sends people out!

• Statement: The whole world needs this hope that only Jesus can provide.

• Bible passage: v5: "You answer us with awesome and righteous deeds,
    God our Savior,
the hope of all the ends of the earth
    and of the farthest seas"

### therefore, go

• Who goes? Doesn’t need to be the smartest … doesn’t need to be godliest (Paul — worst of sinners — biggest missionary) ; what’s needed? Faith in God, a big view of God’s sovereignty, and deep concern for mankind’s need


### therefore, support those who go

• As a church we support people working overseas who are seeking to share the news of Jesus with more people—Christians seeking to make disciples throughout the world.

• There is a lot of truth in the saying that the nations have come to us, but we still need to go because not all nations have come.
• Advice for aspiring ministers and missionaries: don't do it everything someday, do something everyday. Slow build.
• Jesus view was of ambitious growth; told 11 men to go make disciples of all nations.
• the kingdom of god like a mustard seed; starts tiny and grows to be the biggest of all plants
• Grices and Denhams in Taiwan, Wilsons in Italy, Fields in Argentina, Lovells in Bolivia, Skeochs in Papua New Guinea, Chloe in Spain, Gillian in France, Nima and Paniz reaching Farsi-speakers all over the globe, Roselyn Nicolle supporting Bible translators.
• Statistics on how the gospel is faring in different places?

We need to embrace the challenge of mission. Psalm 65 gives us this big view of God, and we need that to endure the small, nitty-gritty tasks involved in doing missionary support well. It's only when you see the beauty of the gospel that you can also see the ordinariness and difficulty of missionary work as beautful too. How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news?

• My friend after college went overseas to an unreached place and he gathered everyone in our cohort and said, "I'm about to go down a well— but if I'm going to go down, I need you to hold the rope." This is what we do when support missionaries through our giving, through our hospitality, through our care, and through our prayer. Let's pray now.
